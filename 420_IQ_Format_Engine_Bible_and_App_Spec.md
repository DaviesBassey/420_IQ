# 420 IQ
## Production-Ready Format Engine Bible & Interactive App Specification

**Tagline:** How High Is Your Knowledge?  
**Format category:** Studio-based factual-entertainment knowledge game show with interview, comedy and viewer play-along elements  
**Season:** 13 × 38–44 minutes  
**Primary platforms:** YouTube and premium streaming; reformatted for TikTok, Instagram Reels, Shorts and audio  
**Core premise:** Adults compete to prove what they truly know about cannabis—its science, history, law, culture, health, business and social impact. The programme tests knowledge, not consumption.

---

# 1. Format North Star

420 IQ is not a cannabis version of an existing quiz show and must never reproduce another format’s ladder, wording, lifelines, set, music or reveal language. Its original engine combines:

1. **Unpredictable but fair knowledge competition** through balanced randomisation.
2. **Human story** through a visible contestant arc.
3. **Verified learning** through concise Knowledge Drops.
4. **Strategic jeopardy** through confidence decisions and two original lifelines.
5. **Visual play** through physical and screen-based challenges.
6. **Audience progression** from viewer to app player, leaderboard contender and future contestant.

Every episode must deliver one memorable insight, one emotionally legible contestant journey, three to five surprising verified facts, at least one major reversal, a cinematic final question and a clear reason to watch the next episode.

## Buyer-facing description

Do not lead with “podshow.” The term is useful internally but can make the scale unclear to commissioners and sponsors. Use:

> **A premium studio-based factual-entertainment knowledge game show with interview, comedy and live viewer play-along.**

Optional audience-facing shorthand: **“the cannabis knowledge game.”** The podcast/audio edition is a derivative product, not the master format label.

## Editorial promise

- Adult, intelligent, entertaining and non-preachy.
- Curious rather than promotional.
- Evidence-aware rather than medically prescriptive.
- Internationally adaptable but locally specific.
- Cannabis is the subject; consumption is never a requirement.
- No contestant is rewarded for consuming, purchasing or promoting cannabis.

---

# 2. Audience and Tone

## Primary audience

- Adults 21–44 who enjoy quizzes, culture, science, debate and social play.
- Cannabis-curious viewers and informed enthusiasts.
- Viewers who do not use cannabis but care about law, history, culture, business or public health.

## Secondary audience

- Adults 45+ attracted by history, social change and intergenerational stories.
- Educators, professionals, policymakers and industry workers.
- Brands seeking intelligent adult entertainment without dependence on cannabis product placement.

## Tone controls

The host is warm, sharp, curious and able to challenge overconfidence without humiliating contestants. Humour comes from personality, surprising misconceptions and strategic choices—not intoxication, stereotypes, medical vulnerability or unsafe behaviour.

# 3. The Repeatable Episode Engine

**Target duration:** 41 minutes excluding platform advertising.  
**Edit tolerance:** 38–44 minutes.  
**Recording target:** 70–90 minutes to create the master and derivative cuts.

| Time | Beat | Purpose and mandatory output |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:35 | Cold Open | Best reversal, laugh, conflict or final-question fragment; never spoil outcome. |
| 00:35–00:55 | Title Invocation | 420 IQ knowledge ring, sonic mnemonic and episode title. |
| 00:55–02:00 | Opening Signal | Host shares a verified quotation, original insight or “High Knowledge Tip” tied to the theme. |
| 02:00–04:30 | Meet the Minds | Introduce contestants, relationship, claim to knowledge, personal stake and episode question. |
| 04:30–10:00 | Round 1: The Mix | Six balanced-random questions; accessible pace; first Knowledge Drop. |
| 10:00–13:00 | Visual Challenge 1 | Timeline, map, macro image, label, artefact or misinformation screen. |
| 13:00–17:00 | The Deep Cut | Short interview beat that activates the contestant arc. |
| 17:00–23:00 | Round 2: Pressure Mix | Six questions; confidence choices; first likely lifeline window. |
| 23:00–25:00 | Knowledge Council Insert | 30–60 second specialist explanation or sourced visual clarification. |
| 25:00–30:00 | Reality Check | Law, health literacy, public safety, ethics or responsible-business scenario. |
| 30:00–35:00 | Round 3: Climb | Five questions; higher average difficulty; steal opportunity. |
| 35:00–38:30 | The 420 Decision | Contestant chooses category and risk band; final question requires reasoning. |
| 38:30–40:15 | Resolution | Explain answer, award points/reward/status and update leaderboard. |
| 40:15–41:00 | Closing Signal | Return to opening thought; preview next conflict/category; audience QR call-to-action. |

## Segment rule

Every competition round uses a controlled mix of easy, intermediate and tough questions. Difficulty is not shown until the question is committed to the contestant. Categories may be revealed first when a strategic choice is part of the episode.

## Pacing rules

- No more than three conventional multiple-choice questions without a visual, story, decision or humour beat.
- Knowledge Drops appear after selected questions only—normally three to five per episode.
- No interview block runs longer than four minutes without a question, visual or active choice.
- Every act-out should contain an unresolved answer, lifeline decision, score swing or personal question.
- At least six moments per episode must be designed for a clean 9:16 extraction.

# 4. Contestant Arc Engine

A score is not a story. Producers assign each contestant or team one primary arc before the episode pack is locked. The arc determines interview questions, category emphasis, reversals, visual challenges and the closing callback; it never changes question correctness or fairness.

## Best-fit arcs

| Arc | Best contestants | Opening claim | Midpoint pressure | Satisfying resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Confidence vs Knowledge | Loud enthusiast, creator, celebrity | “I know this subject.” | A basic misconception is exposed. | Humility, recovery or a bold final stand. |
| Expert Outside the Lab | Scientist, lawyer, historian, clinician | Specialist authority | Culture or slang tests breadth. | Shows expertise plus curiosity beyond discipline. |
| Generational Bridge | Gen Z with 40+/60+ adult | “Our generation understands it better.” | Each fails in the other’s knowledge world. | Mutual respect or strategic collaboration. |
| Partners Under Pressure | Couple, siblings, friends | “We trust each other.” | Conflicting answers and lifeline choice. | Trust is validated, renegotiated or humorously tested. |
| Myth to Evidence | Former sceptic or converted believer | Strong inherited belief | Evidence complicates their position. | More nuanced understanding, not forced conversion. |
| Culture vs Science | Artist/creator against technical guest | Lived culture versus formal evidence | Both confront a blind spot. | Combined knowledge beats tribal certainty. |
| Local Hero | Budtender, advocate, community educator | Represents a place/community | National/global context challenges local knowledge. | Earns recognition or brings insight home. |
| Underdog Climb | Newcomer paired against veteran | “I may not know enough.” | Accessible reasoning produces an upset. | Confidence and qualification grow. |
| Reputation on the Line | Celebrity, executive, public personality | Public confidence | Tough category risks embarrassment. | Grace, humour and courage matter as much as score. |
| Redemption Return | High-potential previous contestant | A known earlier mistake | Similar pressure reappears in a new form. | Demonstrable learning and championship qualification. |
| Ethical Crossroads | Entrepreneur, marketer, retailer | Commercial ambition | Safety/ethics scenario creates tension. | Chooses credible long-term thinking over easy hype. |
| Keeper of Memory | Elder, historian, cultural custodian | Lived or inherited history | Sources conflict with popular narratives. | Preserves complexity and passes knowledge forward. |

## Arc selection checklist

Choose an arc only when producers can answer yes to all five:

1. Can the contestant state a clear opening belief or goal in one sentence?
2. Can the question categories test that belief naturally?
3. Is there a plausible midpoint reversal that does not require manipulation?
4. Can the closing result mean more than winning points?
5. Can the arc be promoted without misrepresenting the contestant?

## Casting balance across 13 episodes

Avoid repeating the same arc more than twice. Across the season include at least: three relationship/team arcs, three expertise collisions, two generational or cultural bridges, two underdog/redemption stories, one ethical-business story and one championship synthesis.

# 5. Question Architecture and Balanced Randomisation

## Question domains

| Domain | Recommended season share | Examples |
|---|---:|---|
| Science & Plant Literacy | 18% | Anatomy, cannabinoids, terpenes, evidence literacy |
| History & Global Roots | 16% | Ancient use, trade, prohibition, reform |
| Africa & Indigenous Knowledge | 12% | Regional history, terminology, colonial law, cultural context |
| Law & Policy | 12% | Jurisdiction-and-date-specific rules, rights, regulation |
| Public Health & Safety | 12% | Risk, impairment, storage, label literacy, claim quality |
| Culture & Media | 12% | Music, film, language, art, representation |
| Business & Ethics | 10% | Retail, branding, agriculture, labour, marketing |
| Future & Innovation | 8% | Research, materials, technology, policy scenarios |

## Difficulty model

- **Spark (Easy):** 100 points. Broad adult knowledge; no loss for an incorrect answer.
- **Flame (Intermediate):** 250 points. Applied or contextual knowledge; −50 for an incorrect answer.
- **Inferno (Tough):** 500 points. Multi-step reasoning or specialist knowledge; opponent may attempt a 200-point steal.
- **Wild 420:** 420 points. A transparent special mechanic drawn from an approved list, never invented live.

Difficulty is assigned during editorial review using pilot testing, not producer instinct alone. Track player accuracy by question and recalibrate after each recording block.

## Balanced sequence rule

“Random” means unpredictable to contestants, not mathematically unconstrained. The app generates each contestant lane from a locked episode pack with these constraints:

- Equal number of questions per contestant/team.
- Equal total target difficulty weight within ±5%.
- Category exposure difference no greater than one question per domain.
- No more than two questions of the same difficulty consecutively.
- At least one Spark in the first three questions.
- At least one Inferno in each main game after the warm-up.
- No duplicate question, answer set, source fact or near-duplicate concept.
- Similar average reading time and comparable visual complexity between lanes.
- Sensitive legal/medical questions distributed equally.
- Questions used in rehearsal, prior episodes or the public app are excluded.
- Seeded generation creates a reproducible audit trail.

## Recommended sequence algorithm

1. Producer selects format template, number of players, episode theme and approved question-pack version.
2. System filters out ineligible, stale, exposed or jurisdiction-mismatched questions.
3. Constraint solver creates 50–200 candidate sequences using a cryptographically strong seed.
4. Each sequence receives a balance score for difficulty, categories, reading time, sensitivity and visuals.
5. System selects the highest-scoring sequence; producer sees a balance report, not a “pick the easier pack” option.
6. Editorial lead approves and locks the pack. Any replacement reruns balance validation and is logged.
7. Game-day runtime uses the immutable pack and records every state transition.

## Question record minimum

Every record contains: ID; version; status; domain; subdomain; difficulty; difficulty confidence; type; stem; choices; correct answer; accepted variants; explanation; short Knowledge Drop; source title; source URL/identifier; publication date; accessed date; jurisdiction; “correct as of” date; sensitivity flags; Council reviewer; review date; expiry date; estimated read time; image/rights data; episode usage; public-app exposure; localisation notes; vertical-clip hook; and producer notes.

# 6. Core Mechanics

## Confidence Lock

For selected Flame or Inferno questions, contestants choose before answering:

- **Curious:** standard points; standard consequence.
- **Confident:** 1.5× points; 1.5× penalty.
- **Certain:** 2× points; opponent receives a steal if wrong.

Use whole-number scores in the interface. Confidence is a story and reaction mechanic, not a constant interruption; maximum three uses per contestant per episode.

## Steal Window

After a missed eligible Inferno question, the opponent receives one ten-second attempt for 200 points. They cannot use a lifeline. The host must complete the original answer reveal before inviting a steal when ambiguity could leak clues.

## The 420 Decision

The leader chooses one of four sealed categories and a risk band:

- **Hold:** 250 points; lower complexity.
- **Rise:** 500 points; standard final difficulty.
- **Reach:** 1,000 points; reasoning-heavy championship level.

The trailing contestant receives first choice only when the episode-specific comeback rule is announced before play. The final cannot reduce a contestant below zero.

# 7. Two Signature Lifelines

Each contestant/team receives both once per game. Lifelines are visually and sonically distinct and become locked once activated.

## Lifeline A — The Trusted Circle

This replaces an uncontrolled “call a friend who smokes” mechanic.

Before filming, each contestant nominates two adult contacts who use, work with or are genuinely knowledgeable about cannabis. Both provide recorded-consent clearance and complete a no-search briefing. At activation, the app randomly selects one available contact. They join by a producer-controlled audio/video link, receive the question and options, and have 25 seconds to advise. The contestant then has 10 seconds to lock an answer.

Why it works:

- Preserves the humour and intimacy of calling “the friend who knows.”
- Removes the implication that consumption equals expertise.
- Provides a backup contact and broadcast control.
- Enables clean timing, consent, delay and technical rehearsal.

Rules: adult only; no active consumption on camera; no web search; producer delay/mute; no unverified medical or legal advice; substitute contact if unavailable; if both links fail, the lifeline converts automatically to **Circle Consensus**, revealing how the verified studio audience voted.

Host line: “You trust their knowledge. Now let’s see whether the Circle can lift your IQ.”

## Lifeline B — Source Signal

The screen presents three short evidence signals related to the question:

1. A statement grounded in the verified source.
2. A common but unreliable belief.
3. A plausible distractor.

Only one is labelled “verified” after the contestant selects which signal to trust. It provides a clue, not the answer. The contestant receives 20 seconds to decide.

Purpose: teaches source literacy, creates a visual deduction beat and remains consistent across countries. For complex subjects the Knowledge Council must approve all three signals.

# 8. Knowledge Drops: The Safe Evolution of Fun Facts

Keep the pleasure of fun facts but retire “fun fact” as the default editorial label. Use **Knowledge Drop** for the on-air feature. Humorous or light facts may be called **Quick Hit** in social edits.

A Knowledge Drop is a 15–30-second verified payoff containing:

- The correct answer in plain language.
- One surprising piece of context.
- A qualifier when evidence, law or terminology varies.
- A visible source cue and “correct as of” date for law/current affairs.

## Safety tiers

- **Tier 1—Stable:** definitions, documented artefacts, basic botany. Standard editorial review.
- **Tier 2—Contextual:** contested history, culture, indigenous knowledge. Specialist/context review.
- **Tier 3—Sensitive:** medicine, mental health, pregnancy, impairment, dosing, law or product safety. Qualified reviewer plus cautious language and jurisdiction/date labels.

Never turn association into causation, preliminary research into treatment advice, traditional use into proven efficacy, or one jurisdiction’s law into a universal rule. The host does not diagnose, prescribe, recommend dosage or tell viewers what product to buy.

# 10. Visual Variety and Production Language

## Five visual zones

1. **The Knowledge Ring:** Main competitive stage; circular luminous architecture and integrated physical screens.
2. **The Signal Tunnel:** Cinematic contestant entrance with tracked light and name reveal.
3. **The Deep Cut Lounge:** Warmer, intimate two- or three-shot interview space.
4. **The Evidence Bench:** Physical challenge table for artefacts, labels, timelines and screens.
5. **The Council Portal:** Remote or in-studio expert insert framed as evidence, not authority theatre.

The venue may host Season One, but no venue logo, retail shelving or product display should dominate the master shots.

## Visual identity system

- **Deep amber:** insight, warmth and reveal.
- **Ultraviolet:** uncertainty, strategy and culture.
- **Polished metallic graphite:** status, technology and neutrality.
- **Controlled off-white:** legibility and evidence.
- Avoid the default “leaf everywhere” visual cliché.

### Luminous 420 IQ ring

The ring is the brand’s hero device: it opens during entrance, segments by question difficulty, pulses when an answer locks, fractures visually on a wrong answer and completes around the trophy at victory. It must work as a set piece, motion graphic, app loader, badge and social avatar.

## Shot variety minimum

Each episode includes: entrance wide; host hero; two contestant singles; reaction iso; score/ring insert; overhead Evidence Bench; audience reaction; moving lounge two-shot; Council Portal; trophy beauty shot; dedicated vertical camera; and clean plates for graphics.

# 11. Audience Play-Along and Community Loop

Viewers scan the on-screen QR code or open the app to:

- Join the current episode with a time-limited code.
- Answer simultaneously without revealing the studio answer early.
- Build a personal IQ score and category profile.
- Challenge verified friends in an asynchronous rematch.
- Enter weekly and season leaderboards.
- Submit proposed questions with sources.
- Apply as contestants.
- Vote among producer-approved final categories.
- Earn eligibility for a future audience episode.

## Fairness and safety

- Public scores must separate live, catch-up and practice modes.
- Leaderboards use anti-cheat rate limits, device/session signals and anomaly review.
- No public real-name requirement; safe display names and moderation apply.
- Audience votes are advisory unless rules explicitly make them binding.
- QR codes must remain onscreen long enough, include a short URL and never redirect to age-inappropriate product sales.

## Progression loop

Viewer → registered player → weekly qualifier → audience episode shortlist → verified audition → studio contestant → champion/alumni contributor.

# PART II — INTERACTIVE APP PRODUCT SPECIFICATION

# 18. Product Vision

Build a local-first, production-safe web application that controls studio gameplay and powers synchronised audience participation. It must continue operating during internet failure, prevent accidental answer exposure, produce a complete audit trail and support later migration from a single HTML prototype to a secure multi-user platform.

## Product surfaces

1. **Producer Console:** configuration, pack approval, run-of-show and emergency controls.
2. **Host Display:** question wording, pronunciation, approved explanation, timer and prompts; answer hidden until reveal state.
3. **Contestant Display:** question, choices, timer, confidence, score and lifelines.
4. **Stage/Audience Display:** branded motion states, scoreboard, category, timer and Knowledge Drops.
5. **Fact-Check/Council Workspace:** drafting, sourcing, review, expiry and approval.
6. **Audience Web App/PWA:** play-along, profile, challenges, leaderboards, submissions and applications.
7. **Admin Portal:** users, roles, moderation, localisation, analytics and audit exports.

# 19. Roles and Permissions

| Role | Core permissions |
|---|---|
| System Admin | Manage environment, users, roles, backups and global settings |
| Executive Producer | Create seasons/episodes, approve packs, unlock emergency changes |
| Game Producer | Run show, trigger states, timers, lifelines and adjudication |
| Question Editor | Draft and revise questions; cannot self-approve Tier 3 |
| Council Reviewer | Approve/reject assigned questions and add review notes |
| Host | Read-only access to active host view and approved prompts |

Use least privilege, MFA for production roles, short session timeout, role audit and no shared admin login.

# 20. Functional Requirements

## Question management

- Create, import, clone, version, archive and search questions.
- Enforce required sources, dates, jurisdictions and sensitivity flags.
- Detect exact and semantic duplicates.
- Require review workflow: Draft → Editorial Review → Council Review (when required) → Approved → Locked → Used → Retired/Expired.
- Preview on every display type and aspect ratio.
- Track usage and prohibit public-exposed questions from studio packs.

## Episode pack builder

- Select season, episode, theme, players/teams and arc metadata.
- Define question count, domain quotas, difficulty quotas, visuals and sensitive-content limits.
- Generate balanced random lanes using a reproducible seed.
- Display balance diagnostics and all rule violations.
- Allow replacement only from an eligible pool; rerun validation automatically.
- Freeze an approved version with digital checksum and approval identity.

## Live game engine

Implement an explicit finite-state machine:

`PRE_SHOW → INTRO → QUESTION_READY → QUESTION_LIVE → ANSWER_LOCKED → LIFELINE_ACTIVE (optional) → REVEAL → KNOWLEDGE_DROP (optional) → SCORE_COMMITTED → NEXT_QUESTION → FINAL → COMPLETE`

Only valid transitions are enabled. Every transition writes an immutable event with operator, timestamp, previous state, new state and payload. Emergency rewind creates a compensating event; it never deletes history.

## Scoring

- Rules configured per format version and locked with episode pack.
- Server/game-engine authority; displays cannot calculate independently.
- Manual adjustment requires reason, producer confirmation and visible audit marker.
- Never allow negative score unless a future format version explicitly permits it.
- Support ties and predeclared sudden-death rules.

## Lifelines

- One use per contestant/team; app blocks reuse.
- Trusted Circle roster, consent status, availability, connection test and random contact selection.
- Timed controlled connection and automatic Circle Consensus fallback.
- Source Signal stores three Council-approved signals and reveal order.
- Unique animation/audio cue messages sent to graphics output.

## Audience play-along

- Join by episode code/QR; server time controls question windows.
- Answer window closes before studio reveal.
- Catch-up mode never appears on live leaderboard.
- Friend challenges use question variants or practice pool, not unused studio packs.
- Weekly leaderboard, category profile, badges and eligibility token.
- Moderated question submission with mandatory source field.
- Contestant application separated from gameplay profile and protected as sensitive data.

## Show control and resilience

- Rehearsal mode watermarked and fully separated from live scores.
- Offline studio mode using a local network and preloaded locked episode pack.
- Automatic snapshots and recovery after browser/device restart.
- Hot-backup operator device with read-only takeover until authorised.
- Screen blackout, hold, timer pause and safe generic holding graphic.
- Export printable emergency question/answer pack with access control.

# 21. Non-Functional Requirements

- Responsive layouts for 16:9, 9:16, tablets and operator laptops.
- WCAG 2.2 AA target: keyboard operation, focus visibility, screen-reader labels, colour-independent correctness, reduced motion and captioned media.
- Performance: local live actions reflected on connected displays within 150 ms target; audience internet updates within 1 second target at normal load.
- Availability: studio game continues without public internet.
- Security: TLS, encryption at rest, secure secrets, CSRF/XSS/SQL injection protections, dependency scanning and structured access logs.
- Privacy: data minimisation, age gating, consent records, retention/deletion schedule and export/delete request flow.
- Observability: health dashboard, structured logs, error tracing and cue delivery acknowledgement.
- Localisation: text externalised, timezone/jurisdiction aware, Unicode, right-to-left capable, local date formats.

# 22. Recommended Technical Architecture

For a production rebuild:

- **Frontend:** Next.js/React with TypeScript; installable PWA for audience; separate operator and display routes.
- **UI:** accessible component system; CSS variables/design tokens; motion layer that respects reduced-motion settings.
- **Backend:** TypeScript API (Next.js server or NestJS) with WebSocket/SSE real-time events.
- **Database:** PostgreSQL with migrations; Prisma or Drizzle ORM.
- **Cache/realtime scale:** Redis for audience sessions, rate limiting and pub/sub; optional in early local pilot.
- **Studio offline node:** local service and database replica/export running on the venue LAN; deterministic episode pack and event queue sync after reconnection.
- **Authentication:** mature identity provider or secure session-based auth with MFA for staff.
- **Files:** object storage for approved imagery/audio with rights metadata.
- **Testing:** Vitest/Jest, Testing Library, Playwright end-to-end, axe accessibility tests and property-based tests for sequence fairness.
- **Deployment:** containerised environments; development, staging, rehearsal and production separated.

For the first pilot, a simpler local-first React/TypeScript app with SQLite may be used, but isolate storage behind a repository layer so PostgreSQL migration is straightforward. Do not keep the production system as one unstructured HTML file with embedded questions and answers.

# 23. Core Data Model

Entities: User, Role, Season, Territory, FormatVersion, Episode, Contestant, Team, ArcProfile, Question, QuestionVersion, Choice, Source, Review, MediaAsset, RightsRecord, EpisodePack, PackQuestion, SequenceSeed, GameSession, GameEvent, Answer, ScoreEvent, LifelineUse, TrustedContact, AudienceSession, AudienceAnswer, Challenge, LeaderboardEntry, Badge, QuestionSubmission, ContestantApplication, ConsentRecord and AuditEvent.

Important rules:

- Correct answers are inaccessible to public clients until reveal.
- A used question points to an immutable QuestionVersion.
- Score is derived from ScoreEvents and cached, never stored as an unaudited editable number.
- Legal/medical questions expire automatically at their review date.
- Personally identifiable information is separated from public profiles.

# 24. API and Realtime Contract Examples

- `POST /api/packs/generate` — validates constraints and returns a candidate pack plus balance report.
- `POST /api/packs/{id}/approve` — freezes version/checksum.
- `POST /api/games/{id}/transition` — authorised state transition with idempotency key.
- `POST /api/games/{id}/answers/lock` — locks contestant answer and confidence.
- `POST /api/games/{id}/lifelines/{type}/activate` — validates availability and records use.
- `POST /api/audience/join` — validates episode code and mode.
- `POST /api/audience/answer` — accepts once within server-timed window.
- `GET /api/leaderboards/weekly` — moderated public result view.
- Realtime topics: `game.state`, `question.public`, `timer.tick`, `answer.locked`, `answer.revealed`, `score.updated`, `lifeline.cue`, `display.hold`.

Never send the correct answer in a question payload to contestant or audience browsers before the reveal event.

# 25. Acceptance Tests

The build is not production-ready until it proves:

1. Two generated contestant lanes meet every fairness constraint over at least 10,000 automated simulations.
2. Same seed + same approved pack produces the same sequence.
3. No client receives a correct answer before reveal.
4. Duplicate answer submissions are rejected idempotently.
5. Lifeline cannot be reused and has a tested fallback.
6. Score survives refresh/restart and can be rebuilt from events.
7. Illegal state transitions are blocked.
8. Offline studio play completes and later syncs without duplicated events.
9. Manual score correction records operator, approver, reason and before/after state.
10. Expired sensitive questions cannot enter a pack.
11. Rehearsal cannot contaminate production leaderboard or usage history.
12. Keyboard-only and reduced-motion operation pass the key flows.
13. 16:9 and 9:16 displays remain legible at broadcast-safe distances.
14. Audience live, catch-up and practice scores remain separate.
15. Moderator can remove an unsafe public display name without deleting the player’s private score.
